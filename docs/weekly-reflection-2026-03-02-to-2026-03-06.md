B. New Experiences

This week brought meaningful hands-on experience in the final stages of system development. On March 2, 2026, I experienced what it means to finalize a system end-to-end, moving from active development into a deployment-ready state. This involved reviewing all core modules of the Tool and Resources Allocation system, validating key workflows, and confirming that the system configuration was aligned with deployment requirements. This experience taught me how important it is to understand the expected behavior of a system before attempting to fix deviations from it.

C. Skills and knowledge areas that you need to improve

This week highlighted several areas where I still need to develop stronger skills. My ability to trace and reproduce bugs efficiently needs improvement, as some issues took longer than expected to isolate. A stronger foundation in debugging techniques and systematic problem analysis would help me resolve defects more quickly and with greater confidence.

I also recognized the need to improve my understanding of regression testing. While I performed regression checks after applying fixes, I was not always certain which parts of the system were at risk of being affected by a given change. Learning to identify dependency chains and anticipate side effects would make my regression checks more thorough and reliable.

Additionally, I need to improve my documentation habits during bug-fixing activities. Taking more structured notes on what was changed, why it was changed, and how the fix was verified would make it easier to communicate progress and support future maintenance of the system.

D. Plans for improving your performance

To improve my performance, I plan to study debugging strategies more intentionally, including techniques for isolating variables and narrowing down root causes in a systematic way. Applying these techniques consistently during future development and testing cycles will help me work through defects more efficiently.

I also plan to build a better personal checklist for regression testing, based on the experience I gained this week. Having a structured reference that maps system features to their dependencies will help me avoid overlooking potentially affected areas when applying fixes.

To address the documentation gap, I will start keeping a brief daily log during active development periods that captures key changes, decisions, and verification steps. This habit will improve both my individual performance and the overall traceability of work done on the system.

Finally, I plan to seek feedback from more experienced developers on the fixes I implemented this week to understand whether there were better approaches and to learn from any missed considerations.

E. Situations you observed during the week that were interesting and might be helpful to you in your professional development

One of the more interesting observations from this week was how a small logic error in one part of the allocation workflow could produce unexpected behavior in multiple unrelated screens. This highlighted how deeply interconnected the different parts of the system are and reinforced the importance of understanding the full data flow before making any isolated fix. It was a practical reminder that fixing a symptom without understanding the cause often leads to recurring or shifted problems.

I also observed that the QA checklist served as more than just a testing guide. It functioned as a communication tool between the development side and the testing side, making expectations explicit and reducing misunderstandings about what correct behavior should look like. This was a useful perspective shift that I intend to carry into future projects.

Another observation was the value of incremental verification. Rather than applying multiple fixes at once and then retesting, addressing one issue at a time and confirming the result before moving to the next made it easier to track progress and avoid introducing new instability. This approach slowed things down slightly in the short term but reduced overall rework.
